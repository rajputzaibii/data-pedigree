How it works

Three checks, in a few seconds

Most journals ask authors to say where their data is. That sentence is easy to miss and often vague. Data Pedigree finds it and reads it for you.

  1. Find the paper

    We look up the DOI or title in Crossref, the official register of scholarly papers, to get the right article.

  2. Read the data statement

    For open-access papers we read the full text from Europe PMC and pick out the data availability section and any repository links.

  3. Check dataset records

    We search DataCite for datasets officially linked to the paper. This often works even when the paper itself is behind a paywall.

Results guide

What each answer means

✓ Data available

You can get the data now

The data is in a public repository such as Zenodo, Dryad, NCBI or OSF, or in the paper's supplementary files. You get a direct link.

"Reads are deposited in the SRA under PRJNA…"

! On request

You have to ask the authors

The data isn't public. Many such requests go unanswered, so we help you write a short, polite request email to send yourself.

"Available from the corresponding author on reasonable request."

✕ Not available

No public data

There is no statement, or the data is restricted by a licence, a company or privacy rules. We tell you which, and you can still ask.

"Data cannot be shared due to participant privacy."

FAQ

Questions

Does this tell me if a paper's results are true?

No. It tells you whether the data is available, so that you or others can check the results. Shared data is a good sign of transparency, but it doesn't prove the analysis is correct.

Why does it say "Couldn't tell" for some papers?

We can only read the full text of open-access papers in Europe PMC, which covers most life-science and many other fields. For paywalled papers we can only check registered dataset records. When we can't read the paper, we say so instead of guessing, and show you where to look yourself.

Where do I find a paper's DOI?

It's printed on the first page of the paper, usually near the journal name, and starts with "10.". You can also copy the doi.org link from the journal's website. If you only have the title, paste that instead.

Does this website send emails for me?

No. It writes the email, and it opens in your own email app so it comes from your address. You read and edit it before you send it. Authors are more likely to reply to a real person than to an automatic message.

Do you store my searches or my details?

No. There's no account and no server. Your search goes straight from your browser to public databases. The name and university you type in the email form are only remembered in your own browser so you don't have to type them again.

Which fields does it work for?

All of them. It recognises general repositories like Zenodo, Figshare, Dryad, OSF and Dataverse, plus field-specific ones such as NCBI, ENA, GEO, the Protein Data Bank, GBIF, OpenNeuro, ICPSR and the UK Data Service.
